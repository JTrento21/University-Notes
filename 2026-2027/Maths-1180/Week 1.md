Vectors and Matrixes

Vectors are quantities that have both magnitude and direction, some examples are:

- Displacement
- Force
- Velocity

Scalars are quantities that are defined by their magnitude alone, some examples being:

- Temperature
- Age
- Distance
- Speed

Scalar multiplication

scalar multiplication involves multiplying a vector by a single number. Every component is multiplied by that scalar.

V = (-2,1)
2v = 2(-2,1) = (-4,2)

Multiplying a vector by 2 doubles its magnitude without changing its direction. Multiplying by a negative scalar reverses its direction.

Zero Vector

A zero vector contains only zeros:

0 = (0,0)

subtracting a vector from itself produces the zero vector.

v-v = 0

Zero vectors have no defined direction.

Scalar product (dot product)

The scalar product is a method of multiplying two vectors, it is also known as the dot product or the inner product. Unlike vector addition, the result is a single number rather than another vector.

Formula:
$$
u=(u_1,u_2,u_3)
$$
$$
v=(v_1,v_2,v_3)
$$
The dot product is:
$$
u.v = u_1v_1+u_2v_2+u_3v_3
$$

Magnitude of a vector

The magnitude represents the length or size of a vector. It is calculated using Pythagoras' theorem.

Formula for a 2d vector:

u = (a,b)

its magnitude is:
$$
|u| = \sqrt{a^2+b^2}
$$
For a 3d vector its: 
$$
|u|=\sqrt{a^2+b^2+c^2}
$$
The general definition uses the dot product:
$$
|u|=\sqrt{u.u}
$$

Unit vectors

A unit vector is a vector with a magnitude of exactly 1. You can convert any non-zero vector into a unit vector by dividing it by its magnitude. The process is called normalisation.
